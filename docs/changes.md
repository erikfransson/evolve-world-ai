# Changes

What changed in each version, newest first, so release notes for any range (say 0.1.1 to 0.2.0) can be put together from here.
Each release is a pull request from `development` into `main`, tagged `vX.Y.Z` on `main`.
Add notes under "Unreleased" as work lands on `development`, and turn that heading into the version number at release time.

## Unreleased

- CI: the test step runs again once when a test worker crashes natively and nothing else failed, so a runner crash no longer blocks the site deploy.
- Live: the Six Realms trails each have their own course and look (Erik: "make 6 unique pathways", the same zigzag on all six looked artificial): the Bloom's a broad S and the widest, the Steppe's one long bow, the Flats' an almost straight causeway, the Mire's a meander, the Crags' a dogleg that narrows at the jog, the Blight's a slow drift that runs straight into its gate. Each worn bed is tinted towards its realm's colour. The trail ends, the realms, rocks and lava flows are as before, but trail food now lies along the new courses (course length 6,650 to 8,960 u, was 6,860 to 8,640). The map version changes, so promoting starts a fresh world.
- Live: poison on the Six Realms (Erik: "much less sparse in the normal domains and little bit more in wastelands and alot more in blight area"): the five realms and the hub carry a trace of poison (the lowest stored weight, 1/64; before none), the Blight's body and heart the highest (255/64; before 192/64 and 64/64), the wastes as before, and Live's poison cap and start density go from 0.01 to 0.0144. Measured 20 simulated minutes after a reset: about 2,200 poison items in the Blight (0.10.0: 1,290), 1,000 in the wastes (940), 0 to 4 standing in each realm. The item slots grow to 229,950 (poison room 0.072, the admin's poison ceiling, was 0.05).

## 0.10.0 (2026-11-13)

- Live: the Six Realms wastes carry 4/64 food (1.33 times 0.9.0's 3/64, two thirds of 0.9.1's 6/64; Erik asked for 1.4x) and the trails 18/64 (was 16/64, 12.5% more; Erik asked for about 15%). Food weights are stored in steps of 1/64, so these are the nearest steps. The map version changes, so promoting starts a fresh world.
- Maps: a new ground property, winter ground (a layer's `winterFood`, 0 to 1): the share of its food regrowth left at mid-winter, on top of the global winter (`eco.winterFood`). A layer without it keeps the share of the ground below, and the winter food (food times share) blends across a feathered edge like food. The rest of the map keeps the global season, and maps without it are unchanged. The legend shows a Winter ground row for a map that has it.
- Live: the Six Realms hub is winter ground at 0.05 (Erik: "a ground for severe winters ... into center hub"): at mid-winter it regrows 5% of what the season gives elsewhere, with Live's `eco.winterFood` 0.4 that is 2% of its summer rate, and it is back to full each summer. While seasons are on, the Live legend shows a Winter ground row that names the Commons.
- Live: the Blight and Mire walls each have a natural break on the far side, away from the hub, out into the wastes, about 2,270 u (Blight) and 2,150 u (Mire) clear at the narrowest (the trail gates are about 5,500 to 6,500 wide). The break is cut after the rock is laid (a map's `breaks`) and the rock wears down to a thinner tip towards it; every rock disc farther than about 1.5 break radii is as in 0.9.1.
- Live and RL Arena (same founders file): the Grazer founder's rays go from 110 to 140, so it holds its home again. One probe on the 0.9.1 map (seed 1, 6 simulated hours): the Grazer keeps the Bloom and ends at 29% of the population, and all six home lineages are alive at 6 h, though the Juggernaut takes the hub late and most of the Blight.
- Known weak spot: in six probes of this map (three seeds, 6 simulated hours, with and without the hub winter) every run lost a realm. With the hub winter the hub empties each winter and its holder changes, and only the Blight falls (3 of 3, at 100 to 250 min, the Oracle dies out); without it the Juggernaut holds the hub for hours and the Mire and Steppe fall as well. A fix for the Blight is being probed.

- Live time-lapse tool: the capture unit gets its own network namespace and reaches the Live public port through a Unix socket served by a sandboxed `systemd-socket-proxyd` unit; it no longer sees the desktop's X server and ibus bus, host loopback, `/run` (system D-Bus, tailscaled, resolved), the webcam, microphone or GPU, and `clone3` and io_uring are refused. - Maps drawn from afar (the Inland Sea at scales 0.04 and up): the biggest ground patches are cut into 640 px bitmap tiles made when first in view, one bitmap per frame while the rest is painted live, and every zoom tier keeps its own bitmaps (up to 32 MB per map, least recently used tier dropped first). ### Speed
- Speed: The Inland Sea at 10k creatures on one thread went from about 70 to about 41 ms CPU per tick under load (about 35 ms or less on a quiet machine), with results bit-identical:
  - The engine's per-creature work runs in WebAssembly over the resident rows: the physics step (with the engine's own Math.sin and cos), the crowd grid, the collision pair search, the item lookup, and the settle and birth scans. About 1.13x per tick (the coordinator 1.20x with 3 helpers). The Creature code is the reference, and parity, fuzz and mutation-checked tests compare the WebAssembly versions with it bit for bit.
  - Helper threads use the resident creature rows too: with 3 helpers the CPU per tick drops about 1.3x and the coordinator's share from about 37 to 24 ms at 10k creatures on The Inland Sea, results unchanged.
  - Eating walks only the creatures that touch an item and the crowd rebuild skips a species copy: about 1.04x per tick on The Inland Sea, results unchanged.
  - Creature body state lives in rows in WASM memory: about 1.15x per tick on big worlds, results unchanged, about 7% less memory.
  - WASM memory now grows by at least an eighth at a time instead of page by page (each grow copied the whole memory): about 1.14x per tick on The Inland Sea at 10k creatures, results unchanged, about 10% more memory.
  - Faster simulation on big maps (about 1.08x on The Inland Sea at 10k creatures, results unchanged): one array-based collision pass for every mode, item lookups in grid order, keepOut skips rock where none is near, and a faster birth scan.
