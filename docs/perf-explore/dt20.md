# Tick length 1/20 instead of 1/30 (dt20, idea 56)

Study for Erik's decision, not exact: `liveConfig.dt` 1/30 s, 90 ticks per wall second at 3x.
At dt 1/20 the same simulated second needs 20 ticks instead of 30, so 1.5x fewer ticks.
No default changed. `liveBench --dt S` sets the tick length, `--memcomp` rescales the memory rates (below), and the output gains `ecoWindows`, one entry per simulated minute.

## Where dt enters

Already per second (a dt change is a pure integration-step change): turn and drag use exact exponentials of `dt` (`decaysFor`), position and heading integrate with `dt`, energy costs times `dt` (`physics.ts`, `step.wat` through `Q_DT`), food and poison regrowth per tick = rate x `dt` (`itemEconomy.ts`), seasons use `tick * dt`, birth cooldown, ageing start and ageing rate are converted from seconds (`cooldownTicks`, `agingOf`), `maxLifeSeconds`, the server's pacing (`owed`, catch-up), the stats and the frame loop (200 ms wall clock, not ticks), the protocol header carries `dt` and the client scene uses it.

Not a pure step change:

| Place                                                       | What happens at 1/20                                                                 | Compensation                                                                 |
|-------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `brain.shortRate` 0.3, `longRate` 0.01 (`Creature.remember`) | Per tick, not per second: the memory time constants grow 1.5x in simulated seconds    | `1 - (1-r)^1.5`: 0.3 to 0.414, 0.01 to 0.01496 (`--memcomp`, tried below)     |
| Braking (`maxBrake * dt`) and the first-order position step | Linear in `dt`, small error                                                           | none                                                                          |
| Reaction delay: sense, think, act once per tick             | 50 ms instead of 33 ms between a sensed change and the response; each brain also reads its own speed and turn rate, so the feedback loop is coarser | none possible                                  |
| Eating, hunting, collisions, births and deaths              | Decided once per tick on the moved positions; a creature moves up to 4 units per tick (top speed 80), 8 for a head-on pair, against radius 12, so no tunnelling | none |
| `eco.thinkEvery` (live is 1)                                | Counts ticks; 2 would then mean 0.1 s. Not changed in live                            | none needed at 1                                                              |
| `MAX_RECORDED_FRAMES` 9000 per creature (`trajectory.ts`)   | 450 s instead of 300 s of recorded path, so up to 1.5x memory per recorded life        | halve the cap if it matters (not measured)                                    |
| Saves                                                       | `ticksAlive`, `lastBirthTick`, `cooldownTicks`, `tick` (hence the season phase) are in ticks of the saving `dt`; a 1/30 save restored under 1/20 would age and shift seasons 1.5x | not checked in code: start a fresh world, or convert on load |
| Replays and RL (`ppo.ts`, `rl/env.ts`) and `inlandMap` use `DEFAULT_CONFIG.dt` | Unaffected, live config only                                                  | none                                                                          |

## Speed

`node src/liveBench.ts --seconds 1800 --copies 800 --every 60`, mixed case (4 founders x 800), taskset 2,3,6,7 at idle priority, load 19 on 8 cores, one run at a time.
`cpuMsPerTick` is user CPU per steady tick; CPU per simulated second = `cpuMsPerTick / dt`.
Steady population is 2354-2421 in every run (the winter crash is inside the steady window), so the tick costs are comparable per run.

| Seed | dt   | cpuMsPerTick | CPU ms per simulated s | Ratio to 1/30 | Peak RSS MB |
|------|------|--------------|------------------------|---------------|-------------|
| 1    | 1/30 | 10.885       | 326.6                  | 1.00          | 253         |
| 1    | 1/25 | 11.127       | 278.2                  | 1.17          | 260         |
| 1    | 1/20 | 11.291       | 225.8                  | 1.45          | 258         |
| 1    | 1/20 `--memcomp` | 10.398 | 208.0              | 1.57          | 274         |
| 2    | 1/30 | 9.754        | 292.6                  | 1.00          | 258         |
| 2    | 1/20 | 9.642        | 192.8                  | 1.52          | 279         |

A tick costs the same (it does the same work per creature), so the gain is the tick count: 1.45-1.52x at 1/20, 1.17x at 1/25 (the ideal is 1.5x and 1.2x).
Load varied during the runs (wall 385 to 2121 s for the same tick count), so the single-run ratios carry a few percent of noise.
Peak RSS is the same within the GC spread seen in `think2.md`.

Reading:
- Food eaten per minute is the same to 0.2% in all runs: the world's food supply sets the total, as before. Food and poison item counts and the seasonal population curve (3200 down to about 3000, then the winter crash to about 1400) agree.
- Births and deaths: seed 1 at 1/20 is 12% below 1/30, seed 2 at 1/20 is 4% above. The two seeds at 1/30 already differ by 8%, so the churn difference is inside the seed spread. Mean lifespan of the dead likewise (130 against 111, but 116 against 117 on seed 2).
- Consistent across both seeds: mean absolute turn rate +11% (0.70 to 0.78), mean speed -1%, poison eaten -9 to -21%. These are the control-loop signature (coarser reactions: more steering, slightly more poison touched), small next to the `thinkEvery` change (turn +26%, churn x1.5-1.9).
- `--memcomp` is not a fix: it moved the ecology further (births +13% to +21% over 1/30, mean energy 460, lifespan 98 s). The unscaled memory rates at 1/20 sit closer to the 1/30 numbers than the "exact" rescale does, so the memory rate is not the dominant effect and the comparison is not clean enough to justify tuning it. I would not compensate.
- 1/25 lies between 1/30 and 1/20 on turn rate, poison and cost, as expected.
- Limits: two seeds at 1/30 and 1/20, one at 1/25 and `--memcomp`; one 30 minute window with a winter crash in it; no lineage shares.

## Recommendation

Going to dt 1/20 buys about 1.5x less CPU per simulated second (1.45 and 1.52 measured, ideal 1.5) at no memory cost, which is the same size gain as `thinkEvery` 2 but with a much smaller ecological shift: totals of food eaten, births, deaths, population curve and the winter crash stay within the seed-to-seed spread of 1/30, while creatures turn about 11% more, run 1% slower and eat 10-20% less poison. What changes in the code: `liveConfig.dt = 1 / 20` only (plus optionally halving `MAX_RECORDED_FRAMES`); the physics, regrowth, cooldowns, ageing, seasons, pacing and client already follow `dt`. What stays: `shortRate`/`longRate` (compensating them made the ecology differ more), `thinkEvery` 1, the frame loop. What needs care: the memory rates and reaction delay are per tick, so the world is not an exact copy; and existing saves are in ticks of 1/30, so the switch should come with a fresh world (or a tick conversion on load, not written). If Erik wants a smaller step first, 1/25 gives 1.17x with roughly half the shift. This remains his call: the long-run differences (lineage shares, lifespans beyond 30 minutes) are not measured.
